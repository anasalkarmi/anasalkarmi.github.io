01:00:16:00Interviews

Get the reaction while it's hot

Contestants walk off the floor with the moment still in them: the relief, the frustration, the thing they didn't say on stage. My job is to be there when they do, already rolling, and to ask the question that brings it out.

01

Rolling before they sit down

Radio mics fitted and a camera already on the shoulder, so the first unguarded seconds off the floor are on the card. Quick on-the-fly lines in the corridor, then a reset for the proper sit-down.

02

Questions that land

Open questions, a beat of silence, and playing the moment back to them. I push for the honest answer, give them room to react, and know when to stop.

03

In their language

I interview in Arabic and English, so contestants from across the Arab world can answer in the language they feel in, and the edit gets clean lines in both.

04

Big ideas, clear lines

Years of interviews for documentaries and news features taught me to help an expert put a complicated idea into one sentence a family audience gets first time.

05

Shot for the edit

I'm an editor too, so I shoot what the cut needs: clean soundbites, reaction cutaways, tops and tails, and notes on the best lines so the edit team finds them fast.

06

Care comes first

Clear briefings, releases on the day and reading the room. A good reaction never costs a contributor their dignity, and the production's duty of care outranks any shot.
